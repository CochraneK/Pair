A, B, and C are not three parallel alternatives; they form a progression from detecting a phenomenon to measuring the mechanism.

A: Is there a difference?
Are psychosis-related prompts more likely than matched controls to elicit inappropriate responses?
B: Does the response move with clinical information?
Within the same scenario, change one clinically meaningful cue and test whether policy shifts in the right direction.
C: Can this become an independent calibration framework?
Develop minimal clinical contrasts into a reproducible pilot benchmark that can diagnose specific failure modes.

A: Cleanly answer the original question first

A has no clinical “axes.” Its main contrast is simply psychosis-related versus strictly matched nonpsychotic content. The 80 pairs largely inherit the original protocol and remain balanced across SIPS P1–P5 at 16 pairs per domain. Thus, 80 is mainly a design-continuity and domain-balance choice rather than a newly power-optimized unique optimum.

Approximately five consumer products provide broader real-world product coverage while keeping collection and clinician-rating workload feasible. The final product list must be frozen before outcomes are known; it is not a probability sample of all Chinese models.

Priority in A: prompt coverage and match quality matter more than indefinitely increasing the number of products.

B: Add a bounded mechanism bridge inside Paper 1

B keeps A intact and asks whether response policy changes when one clinically important cue changes within the same psychosis-relevant scenario.

The first pilot uses only three axes because they have relatively clear clinical meaning and expected directional consequences while keeping the extension bounded:

AxisWhy it enters B
ConvictionMoving from uncertainty to near-certainty should increase the need for clarification, non-reinforcement, and assessment.
Insight / openness to alternative explanationsLoss of openness to alternatives changes the need for reality-oriented uncertainty and professional evaluation.
Immediate behavioral riskMoving from no intended action to imminent high-consequence action provides the clearest test of when risk assessment or referral should increase.

Why six scenario families? This is deliberately an exploratory feasibility pilot. Each family is crossed with 3 axes × 2 levels, yielding 6 × 3 × 2 = 36 prompts. Six is not a statistically privileged number; it limits clinician burden and lets the team learn whether the manipulation is interpretable before scaling to 15–20 families.

Why three products and three runs per item? Three products form a bounded subset of A so the total remains 36 × 3 × 3 = 324 responses. Three repeated generations estimate stochasticity; they are not three independent clinical replications. Product selection criteria should be specified prospectively, not chosen after A results are seen.

C: Make clinical calibration itself the independent research object

C separates from the original student project and extends B into a methods-oriented study. It adds a fourth axis—evidence/plausibility—because this dimension does not always imply a simple monotonic escalation. Depending on context, the appropriate policy may increase, remain unchanged, or decrease, so it belongs in the more controlled benchmark-development track.

C design choiceRationale
8 scenario familiesReplicate each axis across multiple psychosis-relevant phenotypes so an apparent axis effect is not merely one vignette effect, while keeping clinician review feasible.
4 axesConviction, insight, behavioral risk, and evidence/plausibility capture four relatively distinct aspects of belief strength, revisability, intended action, and external support.
2 levels per axisThe pilot tests directional sensitivity only. Precise threshold estimation would require ≥3 ordered levels on key axes.
3 fixed API configurationsThe immediate goal is to validate the measurement framework under identifiable, reproducible configurations rather than maximize market coverage.
3 runs per itemEstimate stochastic response variation; the clinical replication unit remains the scenario family, not each API call.

The current pilot therefore contains 8 × 4 × 2 = 64 prompts and 64 × 3 configurations × 3 runs = 576 responses. A later full design may expand toward 20 families × 4 axes × 2 levels = 160 core prompts plus a separate nuisance-invariance module.

In one sentence: A establishes whether the problem exists; B asks whether the model responds to the clinical variables that should matter; C turns that capability into an independent measurement problem.
