The original protocol's key reference remains highly relevant, but the 2026 literature has moved quickly.

Key reference: Shen et al., JAMA Psychiatry, 2026

Shen and colleagues tested 79 psychosis-related prompts and 79 matched controls across three ChatGPT product versions, with clinician-rated 0–2 appropriateness and proportional-odds analyses. This provides a clear backbone for the original PAIR protocol.[1]

Why it is a strong reference

  • Matched psychosis/control design rather than a simple model leaderboard.
  • Clinician evaluation rather than purely automated judging.
  • Ordinal appropriateness outcome with interpretable effect estimates.
  • SIPS P1–P5 structure for prompt coverage.

Limitations acknowledged by the authors

  • Only ChatGPT was tested.
  • The rating system remained inherently subjective.
  • Recognition, non-reinforcement, urgency, and resources were not separately analyzed as primary components.
  • Each prompt was tested only once per version despite model nondeterminism.
  • The study used single-turn rather than extended conversations.
  • Rapid product evolution limits the durability of point-in-time findings.

PAIR should therefore not be framed as merely a Chinese replication. In addition to addressing several limitations above, it introduces pair-level confound auditing so that psychosis-related content is not inadvertently changed together with financial risk, action urgency, evidence strength, or other clinically important variables.

Representative work that already occupies nearby territory

WorkWhat it already contributesImplication for PAIR
PsychiatryBench, npj Digital Medicine 2026 [2]Large multi-task psychiatry benchmark with thousands of expert-validated items.PAIR should not claim novelty from being a broad psychiatry benchmark.
SIM-VAIL, Nature Medicine 2026 [3]Clinically validated multi-turn auditing across 30 vulnerability–intent profiles, 9 chatbots, and 13 mental-health risk dimensions.Multi-turn mental-health auditing is already a substantial contribution; PAIR should emphasize controlled psychosis-specific contrasts.
LLM Spirals of Delusion, 2026 [4]Directly compares API and user-facing chat interfaces, tracks 20-turn dynamics, and documents temporal model drift.API-vs-interface and drift are important controls, not unique PAIR claims.
When Chatbots Accommodate, 2026 [5]Infers response policies from roughly 47–48k real-world vulnerable-conversation turns."Response policy" itself is not novel; PAIR-C needs a narrower psychosis-specific calibration signature.
ClinDet-Bench, ACL 2026 [6]Evaluates whether models recognize when clinical information is insufficient for a determinate decision.Uncertainty and determinability must be treated as capabilities, not as failure to give a strong answer.
K-Bench, 2026 [7]Clinician-calibrated, protected multi-turn benchmark across 200 vignettes and 125 model configurations.Broad clinician-calibrated mental-health benchmarking is no longer an open niche.
Current defensible PAIR-C signature: psychosis-specific scenarios, one-clinical-cue-at-a-time minimal contrasts, clinician-defined acceptable response-policy intervals, directly annotated target direction, and symmetric measurement of under-response and over-response. Our targeted search has not identified the same combination, but the manuscript should avoid "world-first" language.
