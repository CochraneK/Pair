PAIR is not three parallel experiments. A, B, and C progressively build a reusable benchmark for AI responses to psychosis-related content.

A first establishes whether a psychosis-related appropriateness penalty exists in real consumer products. B then asks whether models react appropriately when a key clinical cue changes. C turns that logic into a benchmark-development pilot for systematic, repeatable measurement across models and versions.

A is the real-world baseline; B is the bridge from ordinary auditing to controlled clinical contrasts; C is the methods core of the PAIR Benchmark.

A: establish the real-world baseline

A preserves the original protocol structure: 80 psychosis-related prompts plus 80 tightly matched controls, balanced across SIPS P1–P5 with 16 pairs per domain. The 80-pair size is therefore inherited primarily from the original design and domain balance rather than from a newly derived unique power-optimal sample size.

A has no conviction/insight/risk axes. Its core contrast is simply whether adding the psychosis-relevant component increases inappropriate-response risk while topic, tone, requested action, and other features are held as constant as possible.

Approximately five consumer products provide useful market coverage while keeping manual collection, screenshotting, version logging, and clinician rating feasible. Five is a pragmatic coverage–workload compromise, not a statistical requirement.

B: introduce minimal clinical contrasts

B does not replace A. It adds a bounded exploratory substudy in which each scenario becomes a controlled unit: scenario family × clinical axis × two levels.

AxisWhy it enters the first pilotClinical interpretation
ConvictionDirectly represents certainty in an unusual interpretation and supports a clean low/high contrast.Higher conviction should usually increase the need for clarification, non-reinforcement, and reality-oriented uncertainty.
Insight / openness to alternativesSeparates unusual experiences with preserved alternative explanations from loss of such flexibility.Lower insight may justify more careful assessment of impact and professional-support needs.
Immediate behavioral riskMost directly determines whether a response should escalate toward risk assessment or urgent help.Higher risk has a clear expected direction of increased response intensity.

B uses only three axes because its goal is to test whether one-clinical-cue-at-a-time measurement is feasible without turning Paper 1 into a full benchmark. Culture, duration, impairment, substances, medication, and evidence plausibility may matter, but adding them all would change the scope of the first paper.

Six scenario families produce 6 × 3 axes × 2 levels = 36 prompts. With three representative consumer products and three runs per item, this becomes 324 outputs. This is deliberately a small pilot: large enough to test clinician agreement and directional signal, but small enough not to overwhelm the primary study. If successful, B can expand to roughly 15–20 families.

Three products are sufficient for method testing because B is not intended as a second market leaderboard. Their selection rule should be frozen before A outcomes are inspected to avoid outcome-driven selection.

C: turn the method into a benchmark-development pilot

C is no longer merely an add-on to Paper 1. Its object is whether a model changes response intensity in the correct direction and magnitude when one clinically meaningful cue changes, while avoiding both under-response and unnecessary escalation.

C adds a fourth axis, evidence/plausibility. This axis is more complex because stronger ordinary evidence does not necessarily mean stronger escalation; the correct response may involve less pathologization, preserved uncertainty, or no change in intervention intensity. That complexity makes it more suitable for a dedicated methods paper.

Why eight C families and three configurations?

Eight is not a theoretically unique optimum. It is a benchmark-development compromise that lets each axis recur across multiple psychosis-spectrum phenotypes, reducing the risk that an apparent axis effect is driven by one story, while keeping clinician review manageable.

Three fixed API configurations likewise serve measurement validation rather than market coverage. At this stage, version identity and reproducibility matter more than breadth; broader model coverage belongs in a formal benchmark release.

Why only two levels now?

Two levels can test whether the model moves in the correct direction when a cue changes. They cannot precisely locate a clinical threshold. If the pilot succeeds, key axes should expand to three or more ordered levels so model threshold crossings can be compared with clinician expectations.

What should the PAIR Benchmark eventually contain?

Ontology

The psychosis-related scenarios, clinically relevant variables, and contextual dimensions that define the measurement space.

Scenario families

Versioned scenario sets that alter one clinical cue while holding other factors as constant as possible.

Clinician ground truth

Acceptable response-intensity intervals, target direction, contraindicated behaviors, and preserved inter-clinician disagreement.

Metrics

Calibration, directional concordance, under-response, over-response, stochastic stability, and later threshold calibration.

Project hierarchy: the PAIR Benchmark is the long-term asset. A+B and PAIR-C are its first validation papers. New models, product versions, and safety interventions can then be re-tested on the same measurement framework rather than requiring a new evaluation scheme each time.
Still to freeze before empirical collection: the prespecified selection rule for B's three consumer products, the formal sampling rationale for C's three API configurations, and the trigger for expanding key axes to ≥3 levels. These are design decisions, not empirically proven optima.
